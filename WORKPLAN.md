# Two weeks to launch

**17 to 30 September 2026**

---

## The short version

The website itself is largely built. What is left is everything behind it: the part CMT logs into
to add properties, the forms that actually send, and the move onto the real address.

That is **ten working days**, not fourteen. Weekends are not working days, and the calendar in this
window only gives us ten.

It fits, on one condition, and there is one thing we need from CMT.

---

## Where we are today

**Built and working**

The full site. Every page, on every screen size. The way it looks, the way it reads, the way you
move through it. Colour and type have both been checked properly and are settled.

**Not started**

Everything you cannot see:

- The control panel for adding properties and writing notes
- Somewhere for the information to live
- Forms that actually deliver an enquiry to the office
- The move onto cmtrealtors.com
- Google

That is the fortnight.

---

## The one decision, on day one

> **We should not build the control panel from scratch.**
>
> Adding properties, writing posts, handling enquiries, logging in, managing photos: all of that
> already exists, ready-made, and can be running by day two.
>
> Building it by hand takes about two of our ten days and produces something CMT would never be
> able to tell apart from the ready-made one. There is no prize for the hard way.

Settle this on the first morning and the rest of the plan holds.

---

## The ten days

### Week one

**Day 1 · Thursday 17 · Keys and decisions**
Pick the control panel. Open the accounts for hosting, storage and email, all in CMT's name. Get
access to the current website and take a full backup of it. Send CMT the list of what we need.

**Day 2 · Friday 18 · The control panel goes in**
Installed, running, with logins for the people who will use it. A private preview link goes up so
CMT can watch it come together, hidden from Google until launch.

**Day 3 · Monday 21 · The content moves in**
Everything currently written into the site gets moved into the control panel, so it can be edited
without calling us. Change a price, see it change on the site.

**Day 4 · Tuesday 22 · The forms start working**
An enquiry now reaches the office inbox and shows up in the control panel, so nothing gets lost.
The sender gets an acknowledgement. We set the email up properly so those alerts do not land in
spam, which is the usual reason a business thinks its website is quiet.

**Day 5 · Wednesday 23 · Photos and publishing**
Uploading property photographs works end to end. Adding, editing and publishing a property works
end to end. Same for a market note.

**Day 6 · Thursday 24 · Look at it, properly**
A full day with the site open on a laptop and on a real phone, at every size, fixing whatever the
eye catches that the code could not. This day is deliberate, not spare.

**Day 7 · Friday 25 · CMT sees it**
We walk CMT through the site on the preview link and write down everything they want changed, in
one list. This is also the **deadline for the things we have asked CMT for**. Whatever has
arrived goes in.

### Week two

**Day 8 · Monday 28 · Google and the small print**
Tell Google what the site is and where every page lives. Make sure every old page address sends
visitors to its new home, so fifteen years of search results do not vanish overnight. Add the
privacy policy. Turn on the visitor statistics.

**Day 9 · Tuesday 29 · Go live**
The site moves onto cmtrealtors.com. We prepare the switch the day before so it can be undone in
minutes rather than a day, if anything looks wrong. Then we test every page, send a real enquiry
through every form, and check every old link.

**Day 10 · Wednesday 30 · Hand over**
We sit down with whoever will run the site and show them: add a property, write a note, work
through enquiries. The manual gets written **after** that session, so it answers the questions
they actually asked rather than the ones we guessed.

---

## Why go live on the 29th and not the 30th

Because something always needs a second look, and a plan with no room left in it is a plan that
breaks in public. The last day is the cushion.

---

## What we need from CMT

This is the only thing that can hold us up, and none of it is ours to produce.

**Needed by Friday 25.** Earlier is better, and the first three matter most.

1. **The professional registration numbers.** The site is built around the fact that CMT's work is
   signed by a chartered, registered valuer. Right now that section reads "pending" because we
   will not publish a professional qualification we have not had confirmed.
2. **How Uganda and Kenya relate to each other.** One firm or two? It changes how the site
   describes itself.
3. **Which services CMT actually wants more of.** The site can lead with any of them. It should
   lead with the ones that pay.
4. Photographs of the team.
5. The full client list, and permission to name them.
6. A mobile number for WhatsApp. Every WhatsApp button on the site is waiting on this one line.
7. Confirmed office hours.
8. Real properties, with real photographs.

**If some of it does not arrive, we still launch.** The site was built to handle gaps honestly:
it says what it does not yet know instead of inventing something. But every gap is a quieter
launch than it needed to be.

## Things worth adding to the plan

The original scope list is a good skeleton. These were not on it, and each one stops a launch if
it is found late. They are already built into the ten days above.

| | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **The old web addresses** | The current site has fifteen years of Google results pointing at it. Every one of those addresses needs to send people to the right new page, or that history is thrown away on launch day. |
| **Email that actually arrives** | Enquiry alerts land in spam unless the sending domain is set up correctly. A business can lose weeks before it notices. |
| **Photo uploads** | Photographs added through the control panel work differently from the ones we put in by hand. It needs setting up, and it is usually found on launch morning. |
| **Privacy policy** | The site collects names, phone numbers and email addresses. Uganda's data protection law applies. Worth CMT confirming whether they are registered. |
| **Visitor statistics** | Nothing currently counts how many people ask for a valuation, which is the only number that really matters. |
| **A private preview** | So CMT reviews a real site before the world sees it, and Google does not index a half-finished one. |
| **A backup, and a way back** | Of the current site, before anything changes. |
| **Who owns the accounts** | Domain, hosting, email and statistics should all be in CMT's name, with us given access. Not the other way round. |

## What "finished" looks like

- [ ] Every page loads on cmtrealtors.com, securely
- [ ] Every old web address still takes you somewhere sensible
- [ ] A test enquiry arrives in the office inbox, and in the control panel
- [ ] The automatic reply arrives, and not in spam
- [ ] CMT can add a property and publish it without calling us
- [ ] Google can see the site and has been told where everything is
- [ ] Valuation requests are being counted
- [ ] Privacy policy is live
- [ ] Backups running, and one restore actually tested
- [ ] Every account in CMT's name
- [ ] Training done, manual delivered, and it is agreed in writing who fixes what for the next
      thirty days

## Not in these two weeks

Said now so it is not a disagreement later.

- Real property photography, until CMT supplies it
- The market notes section, which stays hidden until there is a first note to put in it
- A separate Kenyan site, until we know how the two practices relate
- Client logins, saved searches, property alerts, online payments

## The honest read

Ten days is tight but real, provided we buy the control panel rather than build it, and provided
CMT's information arrives by the 25th.

The risk is not the code. It is waiting on things only CMT can give us, which is why that list
goes out on the first morning and not the second week.
